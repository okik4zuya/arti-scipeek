01

What SciPeek does

SciPeek is a Manifest V3 Chrome extension. When you visit a search-results, profile, article, or journal page on Scopus, ScienceDirect, Google Scholar, or one of the supported publisher sites (Springer, Nature, ACS, RSC, Taylor & Francis, Wiley, MDPI, IOP, IEEE Xplore, SAGE, Oxford Academic, Frontiers, Science, AIP, APS, Cambridge, De Gruyter, PLOS, and Cell Press), or a journal page on an Indonesian university site (a domain ending in .ac.id, typically an Open Journal Systems site), it reads the journal titles and identifiers already visible on that page and looks them up against two snapshots bundled inside the extension itself: the Scimago Journal Rank (SJR) data and the SINTA journal accreditation data (the Indonesian national ranking, S1 to S6). Both are offline SQLite databases, queried in-browser via WebAssembly (sql.js). It then renders a small badge or a small edge tab (Quartile, SJR, H-index, SINTA level and metrics, and links to scimagojr.com, scopus.com and sinta.kemdiktisaintek.go.id) next to each matched journal.

On .ac.id sites the extension only acts on article pages and journal pages; it does nothing on other pages of those sites, such as a university's home page.

That lookup — matching a journal title or ISSN already on your screen against a local table — is the entire extent of what the extension does with page content. Nothing about it requires, or performs, a network call.

02

What data we collect

None. SciPeek has no server, no analytics SDK, no error-reporting service, and no account system. There is nowhere for your data to go, and nothing on our side ever receives it.

Concretely, the extension does not collect or transmit: browsing history, search queries, page content, personal identifiers, authentication credentials, location, or any other category defined in the Chrome Web Store's data-usage disclosure. It sets no cookies and uses no third-party tracking of any kind.

The one thing it remembers is a display preference: the vertical position of the floating edge tab and whether it is collapsed or expanded. This is saved in your browser's local storage for the site you are visiting. It contains no information about you or what you read, and it never leaves your browser. Because it lives in that site's own local storage, the site can in principle read it, and it is removed by clearing that site's data, not by uninstalling.

03

What happens on the page, technically

Content scripts read journal titles/ISSNs already rendered in the page's own DOM (the same text your browser already downloaded to show you the page) and pass them, in-memory, to an offscreen document running the bundled WASM database. The match result comes back the same way. None of this crosses a network boundary — it's function calls within your browser, not requests to any server, ours or anyone else's.

04

The outbound links

Each badge includes a link to that journal's page on scimagojr.com and a second link to the same journal's source page on scopus.com; a journal ranked by SINTA also links to its profile on sinta.kemdiktisaintek.go.id. Clicking any of them opens that site in a new tab — a normal, user-initiated browser navigation, exactly like clicking any other link on a webpage. SciPeek does not pre-fetch, ping, or otherwise contact scimagojr.com, scopus.com or sinta.kemdiktisaintek.go.id on your behalf; the request only happens if and when you click, and it goes directly from your browser to that site, subject to Scimago's, Scopus's or SINTA's own privacy practices, not ours. Scopus may ask you to sign in or use institutional access before it shows the page.

The expanded floating badge also carries a small "Powered by ARTi" credit that links to arti.ai.id. It works the same way: nothing is loaded from that site, and it is contacted only if you click the link. The logo image is bundled inside the extension. The toolbar popup additionally links to this project's source code on GitHub, again opened only when you click it.

05

Data retention & your control

Since nothing is collected, there is nothing retained, sold, or shared with third parties — including for the purposes of creditworthiness, lending, or advertising. Uninstalling the extension removes the bundled database and all extension state from your machine. The only leftover is the tab-position preference described above, kept in each visited site's local storage until you clear that site's data.

06

Changes to this policy

If SciPeek's data practices ever change, this policy will be updated and the "Effective" date above revised accordingly, before the change ships in a new version.
