SciPeek annotates journal listings on Scopus, ScienceDirect, and Google Scholar with SJR and quartile data. This page explains, plainly, what that requires — and what it doesn't.

01

What SciPeek does

SciPeek is a Manifest V3 Chrome extension. When you visit a search-results or profile page on Scopus, ScienceDirect, or Google Scholar, it reads the journal titles and identifiers already visible on that page and looks them up against a Scimago Journal Rank (SJR) snapshot bundled inside the extension itself — an offline SQLite database, queried in-browser via WebAssembly (sql.js). It then renders a small badge (Quartile, SJR, and a link to scimagojr.com) next to each matched journal.

That lookup — matching a journal title or ISSN already on your screen against a local table — is the entire extent of what the extension does with page content. Nothing about it requires, or performs, a network call.

02

What data we collect

None. SciPeek has no server, no analytics SDK, no error-reporting service, and no account system. There is nowhere for your data to go, and nothing on our side ever receives it.

Concretely, the extension does not collect, store, or transmit: browsing history, search queries, page content, personal identifiers, authentication credentials, location, or any other category defined in the Chrome Web Store's data-usage disclosure. It sets no cookies and uses no third-party tracking of any kind.

03

What happens on the page, technically

Content scripts read journal titles/ISSNs already rendered in the page's own DOM (the same text your browser already downloaded to show you the page) and pass them, in-memory, to an offscreen document running the bundled WASM database. The match result comes back the same way. None of this crosses a network boundary — it's function calls within your browser, not requests to any server, ours or anyone else's.

04

The one outbound link

Each badge includes a link to that journal's page on scimagojr.com. Clicking it opens Scimago's own site in a new tab — a normal, user-initiated browser navigation, exactly like clicking any other link on a webpage. SciPeek does not pre-fetch, ping, or otherwise contact scimagojr.com on your behalf; the request only happens if and when you click, and it goes directly from your browser to Scimago, subject to their own privacy practices, not ours.

05

Data retention & your control

Since nothing is collected, there is nothing retained, sold, or shared with third parties — including for the purposes of creditworthiness, lending, or advertising. Uninstalling the extension removes the bundled database and all extension state from your machine immediately and completely.

06

Changes to this policy

If SciPeek's data practices ever change, this policy will be updated and the "Effective" date above revised accordingly, before the change ships in a new version.

Questions about this policy or the extension can be raised via the SciPeek listing on the Chrome Web Store.
